# H345 — Palladium Every-Ticket-Wins accepted-entry hurdle vs dispatch guarantee

Date checked: 2026-08-29

## Question
Can a live UK every-ticket-wins postal competition finally cross the H344 hurdle where the advertised minimum prize for one accepted postal allocation exceeds the unavoidable postage cost?

## Candidate
Palladium Competitions: `Always A Winner™️ | Every Ticket Wins | Over £100,000 Worth of Instant Wins`.

The current page publishes £10 per entry, 20,000 total tickets, a 1,000-entry per-person cap, an 8 Sep 2026 draw, an every-ticket-wins claim, and a stated prize range from £2 to £2,000. It also exposes the free postal route.

Royal Mail currently prices Second Class letters/postcards up to 100g at £0.91.

### Accepted-entry screen
If the advertised £2 minimum were granted as withdrawable cash, one accepted postal allocation would clear the postage-only hurdle:

`2.00 - 0.91 = +1.09 GBP`.

This is stronger than H344, where the minimum cash identifier itself failed postage.

## Governing-route test
Palladium Competition T&C 6.12 disclaims responsibility for entries that are lost, delayed, damaged, destroyed, incomplete, unintelligible, misdirected, or otherwise unsuccessfully submitted and states that incurred costs are not refunded.

T&C 6.13 requires ordinary first/second-class post on an unenclosed postcard, requires separate posting for each free entry, says proof of posting is not proof of entry, and voids a free entry if the competition cap is reached before receipt.

T&C 7.1 is favorable once an entry exists: the prize-allocation system does not differentiate between paid and free entries. That does not cure the earlier dispatch-to-acceptance gap.

Hence there exists a rules-compatible state after the entrant has paid postage in which no ticket is allocated at all. Gross return in that state is £0.00, so the postage-only strict floor is at most -£0.91.

## Secondary warning
The live product page also currently displays `This is a demo store for testing purposes — no orders shall be fulfilled.` Because that warning directly contradicts executable fulfillment, the page cannot independently certify a live money-making route. The primary closure does not rely on this warning.

## Result
**CLOSED — NOT SUCCESS.**

The important new conclusion is not merely that another postal competition fails. H345 demonstrates that crossing the accepted-ticket prize/postage hurdle is insufficient. A strict guarantee must cover the entire acquisition chain from dispatch to accepted allocation. Any route whose governing terms allow paid dispatch with zero accepted entry has a negative strict floor whenever dispatch cost is positive.

Full instant-win class reconstruction is unnecessary for this closure because even the strongest possible accepted-ticket payout cannot repair a legal zero-entry branch after postage has already been spent.
