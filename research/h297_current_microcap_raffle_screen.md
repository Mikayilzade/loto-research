# H297 — Current micro-cap raffle takeover screen

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: **CLOSED for the four checked candidates; global research remains NO SUCCESS / NOT EXHAUSTED.**

## Question
Can a currently active hard-capped raffle be turned into strict guaranteed profit by buying every issued identifier and thereby owning every winning ticket?

The screen deliberately uses a stronger-than-real model: assume one player can acquire every ticket with no per-person cap, no sold-ticket competition, no fees, no taxes, no resale discount and no execution failure. If even this perfect takeover returns less than acquisition cost, the real candidate is closed.

## Candidate 1 — Holy Cross Charities 31 Days of Cash 2026
Source: https://holycrosscharities.org/

Published structure: 500 tickets at USD 50 each. Each ticket stays in all 31 daily drawings. Prize schedule is 2x$100 daily on Oct 1-27, then 2x$250, 2x$500, 2x$750 and 2x$1,000 on Oct 28-31.

Exact arithmetic:
- full acquisition cost = 500 x $50 = **$25,000**;
- total cash liabilities = 54x$100 + 2x$250 + 2x$500 + 2x$750 + 2x$1,000 = **$10,400**;
- perfect-takeover return = **41.6000%**;
- deficit = **$14,600**.

## Candidate 2 — NvACD Sportsman's Raffle 2026
Source: https://www.nvacd.org/?page_id=8287

Published structure: 500 tickets at USD 100, 30 daily drawings in October, winning tickets return to the pool, and each daily prize may instead be taken as a USD 500 CAL Ranch gift certificate.

Using the guaranteed stated substitute value rather than uncertain merchandise valuations:
- full acquisition cost = 500 x $100 = **$50,000**;
- 30 x $500 substitute value = **$15,000**;
- perfect-takeover return = **30.0000%**;
- deficit = **$35,000**.

Because winning tickets are returned to the pool, owning all 500 tickets guarantees all 30 prizes under the impossible-perfect takeover model; it still fails badly.

## Candidate 4 — Sister Cities of Nashville Mendoza Raffle 2026
Source: https://www.scnashville.org/

Published structure: only 500 tickets at USD 100; advertised prize is a USD 5,000 trip for two to Mendoza.

Using the full advertised $5,000 value with zero realization discount:
- full acquisition cost = 500 x $100 = **$50,000**;

## Validation / conclusion
All four candidates remain below cost even under impossible-perfect complete ownership. The best is Holy Cross at only **41.6%** gross. Thus no execution, reservation, tax, resale or duplicate analysis is needed to close these specific pools.

This does **not** exhaust the broader finite-pool mechanism. NEXT ACTION: continue only with a hard-capped/reservable pool whose deterministic player-facing liabilities exceed exact cheapest full-acquisition cost, or with a documented deterministic external subsidy large enough to push a near-miss above 100%.
