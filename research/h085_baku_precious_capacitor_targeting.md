## Important rejection / false-positive control
Not every Soviet-looking KM capacitor is valuable. Public reference examples show some KM variants with only silver-level content and essentially zero or ~1 RUB scrap value. Therefore generic `KM` or `ceramic capacitor` identification is insufficient.

Examples:
- `КМ-5 Н30 100x0.01` reference shows only ~0.0038 g silver and effectively zero scrap quote;
- `КМ-5А-Н90-0.15 мкФ` reference shows ~0.0083 g silver and only ~1 RUB indicative scrap value;
- `КМ-6Б-Н90-2.2 мкФ` reference likewise does not by itself establish precious-metal value in the cited database.

This prevents repeating the earlier relay mistake of valuing a family name without exact subtype/marking.

## Deterministic execution gate
A candidate becomes actionable only if all are true before seller payment:

1. exact capacitor marking is readable from seller photos or in-person inspection;
2. the local buyer confirms the exact classification (e.g. qualifying KM, K52/K53 tantalum, niobium, etc.);
3. buyer provides a **binding whole-lot or per-unit/per-kg payout** for the exact candidate before purchase;
4. quantity/weight is measured or otherwise locked;
5. payout exceeds seller ask + transport + testing/prep + tax/fees by a strictly positive margin;
6. buyer quote remains valid through acquisition and immediate resale.

No theoretical contained-metal estimate may substitute for the buyer quote.
