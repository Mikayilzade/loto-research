# H099 — Azerbaijan cultural-export valuation / state acquisition screen

Updated: 2026-08-19
Status: **MANDATORY-PAYOUT SUBCLASS VALIDATED / PRE-ACQUISITION ARBITRAGE REJECTED / NOT SUCCESS**

## Question
Can Azerbaijan's cultural-property export procedure create a strict buy-below-official-value / redeem-at-official-value arbitrage, especially where the state has a preferential purchase right or a mandatory payment duty?

## Primary law
Cabinet of Ministers Decision No. 294 of 29 August 2014, current consolidated text:
- https://frameworks.e-qanun.az/28/c_f_28340.html

Supporting museum law:
- https://frameworks.e-qanun.az/0/c_f_735.html

## What the law actually provides
### Export application is owner-gated
For permanent export, the **owner or authorized representative** applies. The application must state the legal basis on which the owner acquired rights over the cultural property (Rule 6.2.1). The application also includes documents determining value (6.3.3).

The Ministry then decides whether expert examination is appropriate and, after presentation of the object, completes expert examination within 30 days (6.5–6.6).

### Official expertise produces a market value
The expert conclusion must state whether export is possible and must state the object's **market value** (7.6). The conclusion is used to issue/refuse the protection/export certificate (7.4, 7.8).

### Ordinary state acquisition is discretionary
If, based on expertise, the Ministry decides that the object should enter a state/municipal museum, archive or library fund, the Ministry **has the right** to purchase the object from the owner at the price shown in the document determining value (12.1).

That is a preferential/right-to-buy mechanism, not a general obligation to buy every qualifying object.

### Narrow mandatory-payment branch
Rule 12.2 is materially stronger. If a **physical person leaves Azerbaijan to live permanently abroad**, the Ministry **must pay** that person the amount stated in the document determining the cultural property's value, as a one-off payment.

This is a real mandatory-payment legal branch, not merely a discretionary museum purchase.

## Arbitrage test
Candidate construction:
1. locate cultural property offered below likely official market value;
2. obtain official export expertise / value document;
3. trigger state acquisition at that value;
4. pocket the spread.

### Failure of ordinary purchase branch
The ordinary 12.1 branch fails strict guarantee because the Ministry only *may* exercise the purchase right. There is a legal outcome in which no purchase occurs.

### Failure of pre-acquisition lock
The stronger 12.2 branch still does not yield a reproducible purchase-then-redeem arbitrage for a new buyer because:
- the export application is owner-gated;
- it requires disclosure of the owner's acquisition basis;
- official expertise/market value occurs only after the owner presents the object;
- therefore a buyer cannot normally obtain the binding official value in their own entitlement before acquiring the object;
- if the seller obtains the valuation first and then transfers the object, the buyer is a different owner and, unless the buyer is also the qualifying emigrating physical person, the mandatory 12.2 payout condition is not preserved.

Thus the terminal gate remains:

`locked mandatory payout to the post-purchase owner - fixed acquisition price - all fees/taxes/transport > 0`

and the current public procedure does not allow that payout to be locked before irreversible acquisition by an ordinary buyer.

## Important narrow use-case
For a person who **already owns** qualifying cultural property and is genuinely leaving Azerbaijan for permanent residence abroad, Rule 12.2 appears to create a statutory one-off payment at the documented value. That may be a valuable liquidation entitlement, but it is not by itself a new guaranteed-profit arbitrage because acquisition cost/value was not locked against the payout before ownership.

## Museum-fund cross-check
The Museum Law confirms:
- museums may use allocated funds to buy valuable objects;
- museums may buy museum objects at contractual prices using their own income;
- admission of cultural property to museum funds follows expert/fund-purchase commission procedures.

This supports the conclusion that ordinary museum acquisition is negotiated/commission-based rather than an unconditional fixed-redemption schedule.

## Conclusion
H099 validates a **real statutory mandatory-payout subclass** in Azerbaijan cultural-property law, but it does not satisfy terminal SUCCESS for a reproducible arbitrage:
- ordinary state purchase: discretionary;
- mandatory emigrant payment: owner/status-specific;
- official value is not locked for a new buyer before acquisition.

Reopen only if one of these appears:
1. a seller can obtain a transferable binding state purchase commitment/value certificate that survives transfer to the buyer;
2. the payout claim itself is legally assignable after official valuation but before payment;
3. a specific already-valued object/claim is offered below the locked state entitlement with custodian/Ministry confirmation before payment.

## Research implication
The next highest-value direction is not generic museum searching. Search for **transferable already-valued claims/certificates** or fixed public buyback/surrender schedules where eligibility and payout survive a change of holder and can be verified before payment.
