# H257 — EuroMillions cap flow-down × one-external-jackpot-duplicate bound

Date: 2026-08-24
Scope: LOTTERY ONLY
Status: **REJECTED as a strict guaranteed-profit full-cover strategy; cap flow-down mechanism itself validated**

## Question

H002b correctly proved that a terminal €250m cap **roll-down** requiring no 5+2 winner is incompatible with complete coverage, because complete coverage necessarily contains the realized 5+2 combination.

A distinct cap mechanism needed separate treatment: when the jackpot is already at its cap, the amount that would push it above €250m **flows down to the first lower prize rank with winners even if rank 1 itself has a winner**. Could that compatible flow-down make complete coverage strictly profitable?

## Current primary evidence

Official Belgian National Lottery jackpot-cap explainer:
https://www.loterie-nationale.be/nos-jeux/euromillions/a-propos/jackpot-record-comment-ca-marche

It states that:
- the current jackpot cap is €250,000,000;
- when the percentage allocated to rank 1 would push the jackpot above the cap, the excess is allocated to rank 2 (or the next lower rank containing winners);
- this exceptional lower-rank amount is paid **whether or not there is a rank-1 winner**.

This is materially different from the terminal fifth-cap-draw roll-down and is compatible with owning the 5+2 outcome.

Official Spain SELAE current play/help pages:
https://www.loteriasyapuestas.es/es/centro-de-ayuda/como-se-juega/jugar-a-euromillones
https://www.loteriasyapuestas.es/es/centro-de-ayuda/como-se-juega/como-jugar-a-euromillones

Current facts:
- one simple bet is 5/50 + 2/12;
- price is €2.50 per simple bet;
- 50% of collection is allocated to prizes.

## Exact full-space cost

Number of simple combinations:

`N = C(50,5) * C(12,2) = 139,838,160`.

At €2.50 each:

`S = €349,595,400`.

## Important correction / refinement to H002b

Complete coverage **does not prevent ordinary cap flow-down of the amount above €250m**. It only prevents the separate no-jackpot-winner terminal roll-down condition.

So cap flow-down is a genuine nonlinear subsidy branch and cannot be dismissed by the old incompatibility theorem alone.

## Decisive one-external-duplicate upper bound

For a strict guarantee, every legal external-ticket configuration must remain profitable. Consider the legal branch in which there is just **one external ticket** and it duplicates the realized 5+2 winning combination.

Our one 5+2 line then shares the €250m capped jackpot with that external line:

`our jackpot share <= €125,000,000`.

Now make an intentionally impossible, player-favorable grant: give our portfolio **all prize money generated by the current draw's entire collection**, including money that in reality would be reserved, allocated to other winners, shared, or otherwise unavailable to us.

With our full-space stake plus one external €2.50 line:

`collection = €349,595,402.50`.

At the official 50% prize-allocation headline:

`current-draw prize-money upper bound = €174,797,701.25`.

Therefore even this dominating upper bound gives:

`max gross <= €125,000,000 + €174,797,701.25`

`= €299,797,701.25`.

Against our full-cover acquisition cost:

`net <= €299,797,701.25 - €349,595,400`

`= -€49,797,698.75`.

Return upper bound:

`€299,797,701.25 / €349,595,400 = 85.7556196%`.

This bound is deliberately stronger than the real game because it:
- gives us every euro of current-draw prize allocation;
- ignores all lower-tier external sharing;
- ignores reserve deductions;
- ignores execution limits/costs/taxes;
- ignores any local-code dilution;
- can double-count current money already embedded in the capped jackpot depending on cap state.

Yet it still loses.

## Equivalent threshold

After granting us the entire 50% current-draw prize allocation, the remaining amount required to break even is:

`€174,797,698.75`.

With exactly two jackpot-winning lines, total jackpot would therefore need to exceed:

`2 * €174,797,698.75 = €349,595,397.50`.

The actual cap is only €250,000,000.

Thus **one external 5+2 duplicate is enough to destroy the strict full-cover guarantee even under an unrealistically favorable treatment of every other prize euro**.

## Flow-down itself remains interesting

If there were no external 5+2 duplicate, a crude upper bound of capped jackpot plus 50% of our own stake is:

`€250,000,000 + €174,797,700 = €424,797,700`,

or 121.5112% of full-cover cost.

This is not a guarantee or executable strategy; it only shows why the cap flow-down/carryover state is economically nontrivial and potentially relevant to positive-EV research. Real lower-tier pools are pari-mutuel and external winners exist.

## Conclusion

**REJECTED for terminal SUCCESS.** EuroMillions cap flow-down is compatible with full coverage and is a real nonlinear mechanism, correcting an overly broad reading of the old roll-down incompatibility argument. But the strict guarantee still fails decisively: a single legal external 5+2 duplicate pushes even an impossible all-prize-money upper bound to only **85.7556%** of cost.

Reopen only if a mechanism grants a non-shared deterministic subsidy large enough to survive at least one external jackpot duplicate, or if external 5+2 duplication is contractually/physically excluded by an execution-grade hard rule.
