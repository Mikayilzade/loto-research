# H108 — Lotto Texas 2023 full-coverage control

Updated: 2026-08-19

## Why this case matters
The April 22, 2023 Lotto Texas drawing is a real-world control showing that an organized syndicate could operationally print and buy virtually the entire finite outcome space of a major state lottery within the sales window.

Official/current Texas legislative materials describe the operation as acquiring virtually all 25.8 million possible combinations and thereby guaranteeing that the syndicate held the jackpot-winning combination. The official Texas Lottery history records Rook TX LP as claimant of the $95 million advertised annuity jackpot, and the official jackpot-payment page records a cash value option of $57,804,374.37.

## Exact full-space economics under the 2023 matrix
Lotto Texas used 6 of 54, therefore total simple combinations:

`C(54,6) = 25,827,165`.

At $1 per base play, theoretical complete-space cost:

`S = $25,827,165`.

For a complete unique cover, deterministic lower-tier winning-ticket counts for the realized draw are:
- 5 of 6: `C(6,5)C(48,1) = 288`;
- 4 of 6: `C(6,4)C(48,2) = 16,920`;
- 3 of 6: `C(6,3)C(48,3) = 345,920`.

Using the official April 22, 2023 published payouts ($2,015 / $50 / $3), deterministic lower-tier gross for a full unique cover is:

`288*2015 + 16920*50 + 345920*3 = $2,464,080`.

Official cash jackpot:

`J = $57,804,374.37`.

If the cover is the sole jackpot winner, gross cash package would be:

`J + lower = $60,268,454.37`.

Against theoretical full-space base-ticket cost, pre-tax/pre-operations surplus:

`$60,268,454.37 - $25,827,165 = $34,441,289.37`.

The actual official result shows one 6/6 winner, so the realized draw is a historical proof that the near-full-space buyout strategy could produce a very large positive realized result.

## Why this is NOT the project's strict SUCCESS theorem
Buying the full number space guarantees ownership of at least one jackpot-winning ticket, but does **not** guarantee exclusive ownership of the jackpot. External players may independently buy the same winning combination.

With one external jackpot winner, an equal jackpot split would still leave roughly:

`J/2 + lower ≈ $31.366m`,

which remains above theoretical base full-space cost before tax/ops.

With two external jackpot winners:

`J/3 + lower ≈ $21.732m`,

which falls below theoretical base full-space cost.

No useful hard pre-draw rule capped the number of external duplicate jackpot winners at <=1. Therefore the 2023 construction guaranteed a jackpot win but did not establish positive net profit in every legally possible outcome.

Classification:
- historical realized profit / structural edge: **VALIDATED**;
- guaranteed jackpot possession from full cover: **VALIDATED**;
- strict guaranteed positive net profit under all outcome/sharing branches: **REJECTED**.

## Current replication status
Texas changed the law after the controversy. SB 1346 added Government Code §466.1555, effective September 1, 2025, prohibiting a sales agent from knowingly selling or allowing the sale of tickets to persons attempting to purchase all or substantially all possible winning tickets for a draw.

Current 2026 TDLR proposed rules additionally seek to:
- prohibit knowingly facilitating all/substantially-all combination purchases;
- cap sales at 100 lottery tickets to one person in a single transaction;
- require in-person purchases and restrict terminal counts.

- SB 1346 legislative text/analysis: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB01346S.htm

Therefore the specific Rook-style Texas full-space route is **not a lawful current replication candidate**.

## Research consequence
This case remains highly valuable as a control: finite-space buy-the-pot is operationally possible when ticket throughput and rules permit it. The global search should therefore prioritize jurisdictions that still permit mass/simple-combination purchases and have a sufficiently large externally funded jackpot/rolldown relative to full-space cost, while explicitly bounding duplicate-winner sharing and taxes.
