# H355 — EuroDreams exact full-cover / Boost upper bound

## Question
Can EuroDreams' reserve-funded fixed top prizes, especially the €30,000/month Boost, turn complete outcome coverage into a strict guaranteed-profit construction?

## Current structure checked
Official Belgian EuroDreams material gives a €2.50 play with 6 numbers from 40 and one Dream Number from 5. Tier 1 is normally €20,000/month for 30 years, Tier 2 €2,000/month for 5 years, Tier 6 a fixed €2.50, while Tiers 3-5 are variable. The Boost raises Tier 1 to e.g. €30,000/month for 30 years. The top tiers are capped/shared if sufficiently many winning combinations exist.

Prize-fund rules allocate €1.30 of each €2.50 play (52%) to prizes. Current post-2025 material assigns the residual variable pool across Tiers 3-5 after reserve/fixed obligations.

Sources checked 2026-08-29:
- https://www.loterie-nationale.be/nos-jeux/eurodreams/a-propos
- https://www.loterie-nationale.be/info/questions-frequemment-posees/nos-jeux/eurodreams/jackpot-gains-et-resultats/combien-puis-je-gagner-eurodreams
- https://www.loterie-nationale.be/nos-jeux/eurodreams/eurodreams-boost

## Exact cover
There are

`C(40,6) * 5 = 19,191,900`

possible complete selections. At €2.50 each, a one-copy full cover costs **€47,979,750**.

For any fixed draw, the number of covered plays with exactly k matching main numbers is

`C(6,k) * C(34,6-k) * 5`.

This gives:

| main matches | plays |
|---:|---:|
|0|6,724,520|
|1|8,347,680|
|2|3,478,200|
|3|598,400|
|4|42,075|
|5|1,020|
|6|5|

Total = **19,191,900 exactly**. Among the five 6-main plays, exactly one has the winning Dream Number and four do not, hence one own Tier-1 combination and four own Tier-2 combinations in every draw.

## Strongest useful isolated upper bound
Use the player-favourable Boost amount even if a particular draw is ordinary:

- Tier 1: €30,000 × 12 × 30 = **€10,800,000**;
- Tier 2: 4 × (€2,000 × 12 × 5) = **€480,000**;
- Tier 6: 3,478,200 × €2.50 = **€8,695,500**.

The portfolio itself contributes:

`19,191,900 × €1.30 = €24,949,470`

to the prize fund. With 45.21% reserved to Tiers 1-2, and Tier-6 fixed refunds paid before the Tiers 3-5 residual, the residual own-turnover pool is:

`€24,949,470 - 0.4521×€24,949,470 - €8,695,500 = €4,974,314.613`.

Now grant the portfolio **all** of that variable pool. This is the best isolated case: because complete coverage owns every variable-tier winning selection once, there are no external duplicates in this bound.

Gross upper bound:

`€10,800,000 + €480,000 + €8,695,500 + €4,974,314.613 = €24,949,814.613`.

Return:

`€24,949,814.613 / €47,979,750 = 52.0007182468%`.

Deficit:

`€23,029,935.387`.

The tiny amount above exactly 52% is caused by the rounded/current Boost fixed top award versus the per-play reserve arithmetic. It is nowhere near break-even.

## Why this closes the candidate
This is already stronger than the real strict-guarantee problem:

1. it uses the enhanced €30k/month Tier-1 award rather than the ordinary €20k/month award;
2. it gives our portfolio the entire variable Tiers 3-5 pool generated by our own turnover;
3. it assumes no external winning duplicates;
4. it values long annuities at undiscounted nominal face value;
5. it ignores acquisition/operational friction.

External duplicate winners can trigger top-tier caps/sharing and therefore cannot repair this deficit. The exact complete-control construction is thus decisively below cost before any adversarial dilution.

## Conclusion
**CLOSED / no guaranteed-profit construction.** EuroDreams' Boost is a genuine reserve-funded enhancement, but it does not supply enough external value to overcome the roughly 52% wager-funded prize architecture under exact complete coverage.
