# Near-Hits and Exact Reopening Conditions

Updated: 2026-08-29
Scope: strict guaranteed positive **cash** profit, not expected value.

## Ranking method
Candidates are ordered by (1) whether their favourable arithmetic already exceeds cost, (2) how few independent blockers remain, and (3) how realistic and verifiable the missing condition appears. A high rank is a research priority, not permission to wager and not evidence of a current opportunity.

## 1. H334 — Audrey Cash Cow all-ID cash floor

**Why it was close.** The published 15,125-ID vector reconciles exactly, every ID pays at least £1 cash, and a compliant postcard benchmark was about £0.9532, leaving an indicative +£0.0468 minimum per *accepted* entry before labour. Five minimum wins could clear the £5 withdrawal threshold.

**What killed the guarantee.** Postage/material cost is irreversible, while receipt and acceptance are not guaranteed; loss, delay, cap exhaustion or disqualification can leave cost with no allocated ID.

**ONE material reopening change.** **Binding zero-cost digital identifier allocation before any irreversible marginal cost.** This must preserve the all-ID £1 withdrawable-cash floor and eliminate promoter discretion over a compliant allocation.

**Do not reopen for:** a cheaper postcard alone, proof of posting, more attractive average prizes or another postal route with the same acceptance branch.

## 2. H332 — Win A Million Cash Grab full-pool arithmetic

**Why it was close.** Historical prize data reconciles 10,000/10,000 all-cash IDs and £16,819 total receipts against £9,100 postage, a player-favourable 184.824% full-pool ratio.

**What killed the guarantee.** The player could not atomically reserve the 10,000 target IDs. Processing delay, roll-forward of unavailable entries, separate-postcard requirements and anti-exploit powers preserved non-acquisition/voiding branches.

**ONE material reopening change.** **An enforceable atomic full-pool reservation/acceptance mechanism at a total bound below £16,819.** Reservation must precede irreversible spending and bind every target identifier.

**Do not reopen for:** another merely published all-cash vector or favourable conditional-on-acceptance arithmetic.

## 3. H349 — UK Set For Life Super Chance

**Why it was close.** Exact full cover costs £23,009,085 and, with no external top duplicates, returns £26,269,100 (114.168%). It remains positive with two external top duplicates.

**What killed the guarantee.** Three legal external top-tier duplicates dilute the capped receipt enough to create an £893,831.15 deficit. No pre-draw cap of two external duplicates exists.

**ONE material reopening change.** **A binding rule that fixes the Super Chance enhancement per winning entry rather than sharing/capping it.** This directly removes duplicate dilution; a merely higher advertised pool must instead be separately recomputed.

**Do not reopen for:** a routine jackpot increase, lower observed ticket sales or a belief that three duplicates are unlikely.

## 4. H353 — New Zealand Lotto Strike terminal event

**Why it was close.** Exact ordered coverage has player-favourable isolated gross NZ$2,405,166.36 against NZ$2,193,360 cost (109.657%).

**What killed the guarantee.** The portfolio itself supplies D1 and cannot force a no-D1 rolldown; independently, one external copy of the winning tuple shares D1 and produces a NZ$538,193.26 deficit.

**ONE material reopening change.** **A terminal subsidy paid as a fixed, non-dilutable amount per winning Strike-4 entry even when D1 exists.** This single structural change defeats both the trigger and sharing blockers, subject to recalculated amount.

**Do not reopen for:** a larger Must Be Won pool whose trigger still requires no D1, or lower expected duplicate frequency.

## 5. H351 — Massachusetts Megabucks high jackpot

**Why it was close.** At the observed $28.6m advertised annuity, one-copy coverage would nominally gross $32,523,880 with no duplicate, versus $14,118,104 cost, before tax, present value and execution issues.

**What killed the guarantee.** The jackpot is shared without a hard external duplicate bound. Four external duplicates defeat even H351's impossible-favourable stress model that adds 100% of our purchase cost to the jackpot.

**ONE material reopening change.** **A binding hard cap of at most three external jackpot-winning tickets for the covered draw.** The exact safe cap must be re-evaluated using cash value, taxes, throughput and real pool accounting before any conclusion.

**Do not reopen for:** jackpot growth alone, since an unbounded legal duplicate count always remains; this candidate is more naturally an EV/execution study than a strict guarantee.

## 6. H262 — LOTTO 6/49 terminal Gold Ball

**Why it was close.** In the final one-gold-ball state, a unique-identifier takeover would deterministically receive C$68m and crosses the Gold-Ball-only cost ceiling for up to 22,666,666 paid plays.

**What killed the guarantee.** Identifiers are computer-generated in an open public issuance process; other sales and Free Plays can receive eligible IDs. The player cannot certify ownership of the full support before cutoff.

**ONE material reopening change.** **Exclusive atomic ownership of every eligible Gold Ball identifier before sales close.** A hard count alone is insufficient if any identifier remains externally held or issuable.

**Do not reopen for:** reaching the terminal ball, a large jackpot, or an estimate that external issuance will be small.

## 7. H357 — NS&I Premium Bonds

**Why it was close.** Principal survives, so the loss-of-stake problem disappears and nominal floor equals the £50,000 maximum holding.

**What killed the guarantee.** All prizes can legally be allocated to external bonds, leaving incremental profit exactly £0.

**ONE material reopening change.** **A binding strictly positive per-holder payment above redeemable principal.** Better average prize rates or odds do not qualify.

## Reopening discipline
Before reopening any ranked item, archive the new rule/promotion and show in one paragraph how it defeats the **one material blocker** above. Then rerun all other filters: a structural fix can expose tax, timing, capacity, withdrawal or new sharing constraints. Closed packets remain closed under unchanged rules.
