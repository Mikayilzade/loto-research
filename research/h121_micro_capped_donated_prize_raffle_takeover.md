# H121 — micro-capped / donated-prize raffle full-takeover screen

Updated: 2026-08-20
Status: **MATERIALLY CLOSED / NO SUCCESS**

## Question
H118 screened large hospital/charity raffles. A distinct possibility remained: **small fixed-supply raffles where the prize is donated/sponsored**, so the advertised prize board could in principle exceed ticket revenue and make a buy-all takeover profitable.

This packet deliberately searches the micro-capped end of the market and applies a buyer-favorable full-takeover bound.

For each raffle, assume the buyer can legally acquire every available ticket, faces no per-person/payment/system limits, owns every winner, receives every early-bird/secondary prize, pays no tax, and can realize non-cash prizes at the full advertised value. If even this optimistic gross is below acquisition cost, the strict guaranteed-profit takeover is rejected a fortiori.

## General result
A small ticket cap plus a donated or sponsored prize is **not sufficient**. For a deterministic takeover, a necessary condition is:

`realizable fixed prize board + deterministic subsidies > minimum cost to own the complete eligible supply + taxes/fees/execution`.

The six micro-capped examples above span cash-only, vehicle/cash-choice, reverse-raffle, sponsored prize, and multi-draw structures. Their deliberately optimistic gross ceilings are **10% to 71.725%**, all far below 100% before taxes and execution.

This materially extends H118 from large hospital/charity raffles into the small-cap/donated-prize end of the market. Reopen this class only when a verified raffle has either:
1. total fixed cash-realizable prize board **above** cheapest complete-supply acquisition cost; or
2. a deterministic rebate/discount large enough to push that ratio above 100%;
with all tickets legally and atomically purchasable by one buyer/group.

## Conclusion
**NO SUCCESS.** Micro-capped donated-prize raffles can have much better odds than mass lotteries, but the fundraising economics still leave a large takeout. The closest verified sample in this packet, Lancaster Rotary, reaches only **71.725%** even when the car is valued at full MSRP and all secondary prizes are captured.
