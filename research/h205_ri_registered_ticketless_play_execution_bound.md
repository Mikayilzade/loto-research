# H205 — Rhode Island Registered Ticketless Play execution bound

Updated: 2026-08-23
Status: **NO SUCCESS**
Scope: LOTTERY ONLY.

## Target
Continue H204's execution investigation using the current official Rhode Island Lottery Rules (effective February 20, 2026) and current official Keno/app interfaces. The question is whether current Registered Ticketless Play or the mobile digital-play-slip channel proves a bulk mechanism capable of placing the H175/H173 portfolios (4,336 / 4,560 distinct 3-spot selections) into one four-minute Keno draw.

## Fresh primary-source evidence

### 1. Current rules explicitly define electronic Keno play
The February 20, 2026 Rhode Island Lottery Rules define **Registered Ticketless Play** as an accepted iLottery wager placed by an identified player through that player's iLottery account, with the electronic system record being the transaction of record.

The same rules define an electronic Play Slip for Registered Ticketless Play and expressly state that Keno may be played through Registered Ticketless Play.

Official rules: https://www.rilot.com/content/dam/interactive/ilottery/pdfs/about-us/RILotteryRules.pdf

### 2. Current Keno transaction limits
For Keno, the rules state:
- one Keno play chooses 1–10 spots from 1–80;
- wager per draw may be $1, $2, $5, or $10;
- a player may extend the wager to up to 15 consecutive draws;
- maximum price for any Keno Ticket or Registered Ticketless Play is $150 base, $300 with Plus or Overtime, and $450 with both;
- Keno draws occur every four minutes.

The $150/$300/$450 caps therefore remain fully explainable by wager amount × consecutive-draw count and optional equal-cost add-ons. They do **not** establish that one Registered Ticketless Play can contain 150 independently chosen same-draw selections.

### 3. Current public iKeno purchase UI is single-selection shaped
The current official Keno purchase page exposes one four-step purchase flow:
1. Pick your numbers / Quick Pick;
2. Amount per game;
3. Consecutive games;
4. Plus / Overtime options;
followed by a single Buy Now action.

No public control for `add line`, `add selection`, quantity of different number sets, bulk import, or a multi-selection cart is exposed in the current page text.

Official Keno page: https://www.rilot.com/en-us/keno.html

This is evidence about the currently exposed public flow, not a proof that no hidden/internal batching capability exists.

### 4. Current app does support reusable digital wagers, but capacity is unspecified
The official RI mobile-app page says the app can:
- buy/play Keno;
- create and save favorite draw-game wagers;
- make digital play slips that are scanned at Lottery retailers for quicker purchase.

The current FAQ says `My Favorites` can store favorite wagers for all draw games offered online; Keno is currently offered online. The official Google Play listing likewise says the app can purchase Keno, save favorite wagers, and create digital play slips for presentation to a retailer.

Official sources:
- https://www.rilot.com/en-us/player-zone/mobile-app.html
- https://www.rilot.com/en-us/player-zone/faqs.html
- https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rhodeisland.lottery.fenix

This materially strengthens H204's finding that Keno selections can be prepared/replayed digitally. But none of these sources specifies how many **different** Keno selections one QR/digital play slip or one retail scan can contain.

## Exact execution consequence

For H175, all 4,336 distinct selections must participate in the **same** Keno draw. Consecutive-game replication cannot reduce this count because it repeats a selection over future draws rather than creating different selections in the target draw.

If the public one-selection-per-purchase interpretation is the only executable path, a four-minute draw window would require:

`4,336 / 240 = 18.0667 completed distinct purchases per second`

and H173 would require:

`4,560 / 240 = 19.0 completed distinct purchases per second`.

These are not claimed as formal system throughput lower bounds if a genuine multi-selection QR/batch channel exists; they are the lower bounds under the currently documented single-selection flow.

## What H205 closes

H205 closes the shortcut that the current **Registered Ticketless Play price cap itself** proves same-draw bulk capacity. It does not. The cap is naturally generated by the published per-draw wager, 15-consecutive-draw feature, and optional equal-cost add-ons.

H205 also establishes that current Keno is explicitly within Registered Ticketless Play and that Keno-compatible saved digital wagers are a real current architecture, so the correct remaining question is no longer whether electronic Keno exists but **how many distinct Keno selections can be committed atomically/per scan/per transaction to one draw**.

## Remaining gate

A strict H173/H175 execution proof still needs at least one of:
1. official/vendor documentation giving a finite multi-selection capacity per Keno digital play slip / QR / transaction;
2. direct current app/terminal evidence showing multiple independently selected Keno number sets carried by one scan/order and accepted for the same draw;
3. a documented terminal/SSVM import/batch mechanism with throughput and rejection guarantees sufficient to complete the portfolio before draw close.

Without such evidence, the guarantee cannot assume that Favorites or QR codes compress thousands of distinct selections.

## Non-repeat rule
Do not again infer same-draw selection capacity from the $150/$300/$450 ticket/RTP price limits. Future work must recover an explicit number-of-independent-selections/panels/lines capacity or a demonstrated batch transaction.

## Result
**ЕЩЁ НЕ УСПЕХ.** Current 2026 rules formally validate electronic Keno and digital saved wagers, but neither the Registered Ticketless Play price cap nor the public UI proves batching of thousands of different selections into one four-minute draw.
