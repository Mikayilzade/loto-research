# H263 — Spain Christmas Lottery 2026 full-issue takeover cap

## Question
Can the Spanish 2026 Sorteo Extraordinario de Navidad become a guaranteed-profit finite-identifier takeover because the game has a fixed set of numbered tickets/series and buyers can target numbers?

## Official 2026 issuance
SELAE's official 2026 fourth-quarter prize programme states for the 22 December 2026 Christmas draw:

- **205 series**;
- **100,000 billetes per series**;
- **€200 per billete** (normally divisible into €20 décimos);
- total issue value **€4,100,000,000**;
- exactly **70% for prizes = €2,870,000,000**.

Official source:
- https://www.loteriasyapuestas.es/f/loterias/documentos/Loter%C3%ADa%20Nacional/programas%20de%20premios/PROGRAMAS_PREMIOS_CUARTO_TRIMESTRE_2026_SABADOS.pdf

SELAE's rules/help also describe Christmas Lottery first-prize odds as 1 in 100,000, consistent with the 100,000-number space:
- https://www.loteriasyapuestas.es/es/centro-de-ayuda/como-se-juega/jugar-a-loteria-nacional

## Exact takeover arithmetic
A deliberately impossible-favorable strategy is to acquire the **entire issued inventory**, so every winning billete/series/fraction is ours and there is no external sharing/execution uncertainty left.

Cost:

`205 * 100,000 * €200 = €4,100,000,000`.

Because the official programme fixes total prizes at €2,870,000,000, complete ownership receives at most/exactly that prize mass before taxes/friction.

- complete-issue cost: **€4,100,000,000**;
- total prize mass: **€2,870,000,000**;
- deterministic gross return: **70.0000%**;
- deterministic deficit: **€1,230,000,000**.

The same ratio holds for the stronger hypothetical of owning every number in one complete series: the issue and prize programme are linear by series, so a complete one-series takeover returns 70% before acquisition friction.

## Why this closes the mechanism
This is stronger than merely arguing that particular numbers/series may be difficult to reserve. Even granting perfect inventory access, perfect selection, no competitors, no fees, no taxes, and capture of every prize attached to the owned inventory, the fixed prize budget is only 70% of acquisition cost.

Therefore finite/player-targetable identifiers alone do not rescue this game. A deterministic external subsidy would need to cover more than **30% of retail acquisition cost** (and then also any real friction/tax effects) before a full takeover could cross break-even.

## Result
**REJECTED for guaranteed-profit full takeover.** Current official 2026 economics impose a hard 70% gross ceiling.

This is useful for the H262 follow-up class: it is a genuine hard finite numbered issue, but unlike H262's terminal Gold Ball state its deterministic prize mass cannot exceed complete acquisition cost.
